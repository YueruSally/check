# 8. Discussion

## 8.1 Capacity and schedule disruptions are not operationally symmetric

The experiments distinguish two mechanisms that would be obscured by a single generic “port disruption” variable. Capacity reductions changed which West Coast resources were used and brought selected node-days close to their limits, but did not make NY/NJ operationally material in the capacity-only suite. Long sailing delays, by contrast, changed the temporal compatibility of the West Coast paths and made the all-water East Coast alternative competitive. Capacity then amplified that substitution in some compound conditions. Thus an equal-seeming deterioration in two input parameters need not produce an equal network response: capacity affects the amount that can move through a resource, whereas a timetable delay changes which downstream connections remain available.

This interpretation is conditional on the model's fixed departures and candidate paths. Freight cannot create an unscheduled service, and the model does not contain endogenous queueing. The result should therefore be stated as a difference between capacity- and timetable-driven reallocation in the constructed network, not as a universal claim that delay always matters more than capacity.

## 8.2 Gateway activation is a region of preferences, not a single switching time

The configuration-preserving closure changes the conceptual meaning of an activation threshold. Memphis at 264 h contains both zero-share and material-share configurations on the retained non-dominated union. A decision maker emphasizing cost can rationally remain on the West Coast, while one emphasizing time can assign a substantial share to NY/NJ. The appropriate description is a transition region in objective preference, rather than a single physical point at which the network “switches ports.”

This distinction also resolves the apparent destination ordering. Columbus reaches front-wide activation at 240 h, but Memphis and Chicago both do so at 288 h on the tested grid. Memphis nevertheless reaches first-solution activation at 264 h. Inland geography therefore affects both the onset of competitiveness and the width of the preference-dependent transition. Reporting only a balanced solution or an average gateway share would conceal this structure.

## 8.3 Alternative-gateway share and alternative-gateway value are different quantities

The exact endpoint results show why gateway share should not be used as a proxy for resilience value. At Memphis and 264 h, the minimum-cost endpoint uses no NY/NJ. The minimum-time endpoint assigns 32.66% to NY/NJ and saves 2.939 h, but costs USD 0.797 million more. NY/NJ is valuable there as a time-oriented option, not as a cost-saving route. Under a 288 h delay, the same destination obtains both cost and time benefits. The operational value of reserving East Coast access therefore depends on disruption severity and the decision maker's marginal willingness to pay for time.

The shipment-matched decomposition further shows that system time gains need not be generated by the NY/NJ-assigned freight being individually faster. For Memphis at 264 h and Columbus at 240 h, the NY/NJ component is slower, while the West Coast-assigned component improves by a larger amount. The associated service records show offsetting shifts from the Shanghai--Los Angeles/Long Beach departure to Shanghai--NY/NJ. This is consistent with reallocation of fixed timetable opportunities. Chicago at 288 h differs because its NY/NJ component is approximately neutral and the West Coast component supplies nearly all of the mean system gain. The mechanism is consequently destination specific, not a generic claim that the East Coast route is faster.

## 8.4 Managerial implications

Three decisions follow from the results. First, contingency planning should specify an objective and an inland market, not only a disruption trigger. The same 264 h condition supports different Memphis gateway choices at the cost and time endpoints. Second, NY/NJ access has option value before it becomes universal across the trade-off set. Contracts, capacity reservations, and inland connections can therefore be prepared during the transition regime without requiring immediate full diversion. Third, high observed diversion share should not be interpreted automatically as high economic benefit. Decision makers should compare an allowed-gateway plan with a same-disruption restricted counterfactual and report cost and time separately.

These implications do not prescribe a universal diversion threshold. The tested hours are scenario-specific indicators. In practice, an operator should recompute the transition region using current demand, tariffs, available sailings, inland capacity, and contractual switching costs. Such costs are not represented in the present model and could make small time benefits unattractive.

## 8.5 Reliability and limitations

The final validation separates the roles of the two solution methods. NSGA-II maps cost--time structure and preserves multiple found configurations, while the MILP proves selected lexicographic endpoints for the same candidate-path problem. The observed heuristic cost gaps, reaching 4.338% in the hardest validated model, justify using exact rather than heuristic endpoint values in the main counterfactual table. They also caution against interpreting small differences between stochastic runs as transport mechanisms.

Five limitations define the scope of the conclusions. First, the network, demand, timetable, and disruption levels form a constructed case study rather than a statistically calibrated representation of all China--US freight. Second, exactness is conditional on the generated candidate-path library. Third, most capacity--delay grid cells have allowed-search evidence only and remain exploratory. Fourth, the model uses fixed schedule and capacity accounting rather than endogenous congestion, recovery, or carrier behaviour. Fifth, 30 seeds characterize algorithmic variation under fixed inputs and do not quantify uncertainty in demand, prices, or disruption duration. Future work should address those input uncertainties and switching costs only if they advance the substantive gateway-choice question; additional scenario volume alone would not strengthen the present argument.
